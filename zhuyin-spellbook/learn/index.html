What is Zhuyin?

Zhuyin (注音, “marking sounds”) is a phonetic alphabet for Mandarin Chinese. Each symbol stands for one piece of a syllable: a starting consonant, a middle glide, or an ending vowel. A tone mark goes at the end. The symbols were created in the 1910s from simple parts of Chinese characters, so ㄉ comes from 刀 (knife) and ㄌ from 力 (strength).

Taiwan still teaches every child to read with Zhuyin before characters. You will find it printed beside the text in children’s books, in dictionaries and on phone keyboards. For learners outside Taiwan, it is the most direct way into Taiwanese Mandarin, books and media.

Why learn Zhuyin instead of starting with Pinyin?

  • No English spelling habits. Pinyin uses letters you already “know”, so q, x, c and zh get read the English way. Zhuyin symbols are new, so you learn the real sound.
  • It is what Taiwan reads. Children’s books, school materials and Taiwanese grandparents all use it.
  • It builds a bridge to characters. The symbols look like character parts, so your eyes get used to Chinese shapes early.

Try the tissue test

Hold a tissue in front of your mouth. Say ㄆ: the tissue should move. Say ㄅ: it should stay still. The same goes for ㄊ/ㄉ, ㄎ/ㄍ, ㄑ/ㄐ, ㄔ/ㄓ and ㄘ/ㄗ. The difference in Mandarin is the puff of air, not voicing as in English b and p.

The four tones

The same syllable means different things at different pitches. Pitch is shown on a 1 (low) to 5 (high) scale. Tap the character to hear it.

Tone 1 一聲 ˉ

High and level · pitch 55

No mark in Zhuyin. Hold it steady, like singing one note.

Tone 2 二聲 ˊ

Rising · pitch 35

Goes up, like asking “Huh?”

Tone 3 三聲 ˇ

Low dip · pitch 214

Dips low, then rises. In everyday Taiwan speech it often stays low.

Tone 4 四聲 ˋ

Falling · pitch 51

Drops fast, like a firm “No!”

The neutral tone ˙

Some syllables are said short and light. Zhuyin marks them with a dot placed before the syllable, for example 媽媽 ㄇㄚ ˙ㄇㄚ (mom). Taiwan Mandarin uses the neutral tone less often than Beijing Mandarin.

Learn tones with your hand

Draw each pitch in the air while you listen: flat, up, dip, down. Studies on Mandarin learners have found that hand gestures tracing pitch help people remember tones better than listening alone.

Zhuyin vs Pinyin

Both write the same Mandarin sounds. They differ in where they are used and in what trips learners up.

A study plan that follows the research

Five steps, in order. Each one comes from how Taiwanese first-graders are taught or from learning research.

  1. Listen before you read

    Train your ear on pairs that sound alike, such as ㄓ/ㄗ, ㄣ/ㄥ and ㄅ/ㄆ. Hearing many different voices helps, which is a finding from tone and sound training studies.

  2. Start from words you know

    Taiwan’s Grade 1 begins with whole sentences and words, then zooms in on symbols. Learn ㄅ through 爸爸 (dad), not as an abstract shape.

  3. Quiz yourself for 10 minutes a day

    Pulling an answer from memory beats rereading a chart (retrieval practice). Spreading short sessions across days beats one long session (spacing).

  4. Add tones with gestures

    Start with tones 1 and 4, which are easiest to tell apart, then add 2, then practise 2 against 3. Trace each pitch with your hand.

  5. Blend directly

    Read ㄍㄨㄛˊ as one sound, guó. Then move on to two-syllable words and short sentences.

Background reading: Roediger & Karpicke (2006) on retrieval practice; Cepeda et al. (2006) on spaced practice; Wang, Spence, Jongman & Sereno (1999) on training Mandarin tone perception; Baills et al. (2019) on pitch gestures.

Frequently asked questions

Is Zhuyin the same as Bopomofo?

Yes. The official name is 注音符號 (Zhùyīn fúhào), “phonetic symbols”. People also call it Bopomofo after its first four symbols, ㄅ ㄆ ㄇ ㄈ.

How many Zhuyin symbols are there?

There are 37 symbols: 21 initials, 3 medials and 13 finals. Four tone marks (ˊ ˇ ˋ ˙) are added after them. Tone 1 has no mark.

How long does it take to learn Zhuyin?

Children in Taiwan learn it in roughly the first ten weeks of Grade 1. Adults who practise for 10 minutes a day usually recognise all 37 symbols within a few weeks. Reading smoothly takes longer, as with any new alphabet.

Do people in Taiwan still use Zhuyin?

Yes. It is taught in every primary school, printed beside characters in children’s books, used in dictionaries, and it is one of the most common ways to type Chinese on phones and computers in Taiwan.

Should my child learn Zhuyin or Pinyin?

If your family has roots in Taiwan, plans to visit or study there, or wants to read Taiwanese children’s books, start with Zhuyin. If your child’s school or textbooks use Pinyin, follow the school. Many learners pick up the second system quickly once they know the sounds.

Can I learn Zhuyin if I don’t speak any Chinese?

Yes, and it is a good first step. Zhuyin teaches you the sounds of Mandarin without the English spelling habits that make Pinyin letters like q, x and zh confusing.
